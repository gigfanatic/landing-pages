"That's nice."

If you've ever played a song for friends or family, you've heard it. You've probably seen the look that comes with it, too. Truth be told, "nice" is the most dangerous thing anyone can say about your song. Nobody hates it, so nothing changes. A week later, nobody remembers it.

I know because I made a whole album of nice. Shortly after the Shaggy hits, two friends and I started our own label and made whatever music we liked. We never once thought about who it was for. That album cost about $20,000. It went nowhere.

Looking back, I can tell you exactly why. The choruses were nice, and that's all they were. Nothing stuck in your head. I struggle to remember any of them myself, which is a strange thing for the guy who wrote them to admit, to say the least.

Here's the problem: when I hear a song from an up-and-coming songwriter, the comment I give most often is "Hmm, what was the chorus?" That's where nice lives. So that's where this test looks.

Grab one of your songs. You'll have your score in about five minutes.

How to use it

  1. Pick one finished song. Just one.
  2. Play or sing the chorus out loud, once or twice.
  3. Answer each question fast. Your first honest answer is usually the right one.
  4. Tap No, Sort of or Yes for each one. Your score adds itself up.
  5. Answer all 8 to see your band and what to fix first.

Questions 3 and 6 are about strangers. If you can, play the chorus for someone who's never heard it and let them answer those two.

Hearing your own chorus fresh

There's one thing this test can't do for you. It can't make you hear your own chorus fresh.

Nobody can. You've heard your song a hundred times before anyone else hears it once. You know how the chorus is supposed to feel, so your brain fills in the gaps. That's why "nice" keeps happening, even to good writers.

I had success and had no idea how it happened. It took me years to work out why my biggest songs worked: hosting a radio morning show, reading songwriting books, and a challenge where I wrote 30 songs in 30 days. Those songs had been written chorus first. I just didn't know it at the time.
